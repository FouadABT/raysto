4. MOTION IS A REQUIRED DESIGN SYSTEM

Audit and extend the existing motion layer. Use framework Material transitions and motion tokens whenever available. Do not add a separate collection of arbitrary easing curves.

REQUIRED CHOREOGRAPHY

- Navigation into details: preserve spatial continuity; use a suitable shared element/container transition only when the source and destination represent the same object.
- Back navigation: reverse the relationship naturally and respect platform back gestures and predictive-back behavior where supported.
- Top-level navigation: preserve destination state and use the established Material transition; do not play an onboarding-style entrance every time a tab is selected.
- Onboarding steps: animate the changing content and progress together; support forward, backward, skip, and immediate interaction.
- Sheets/dialogs: use Material entry, dismissal, scrim, drag, keyboard, and focus behavior.
- Cart changes: animate the affected quantity, item insertion/removal, total, and cart affordance with restrained emphasis. Keep numeric and screen-reader updates understandable.
- Search/filtering: animate relevant results changes without repeatedly cascading the entire list or moving the search field.
- Order/service updates: update the changed badge, timeline step, or row. Preserve scroll position and stable keys.
- Floor-plan editing: direct manipulation follows the finger; snap on release; selection and inspector transitions must not interfere with dragging.
- AI preview: show brief deterministic progress or typing feedback, then reveal a complete understandable response. Avoid slow character-by-character text that delays use or creates noisy accessibility announcements.
- Payment: show pending until the selected mock outcome resolves; use a brief confirmation only after local success.
- Empty/loading states: use stable placeholders that match final geometry. Stop animations when hidden and avoid endless decorative shimmer.
- Theme/language/account changes: avoid flashes, overlapping shells, stale screens, and animation replay storms.

MOTION RULES

- Input feedback must begin immediately; animation cannot delay data entry, navigation, or critical actions.
- Transitions must be interruptible. Rapid taps must not stack routes, duplicate sheets, or restart conflicting animations.
- Use small stagger only for a short introductory group; suggested delay is 20–30 ms per item with a total cap of 120 ms. Do not stagger long lists.
- Keep decorative movement brief and purposeful. Avoid perpetual floating cards, dramatic parallax, repeated confetti, and bouncing operational queues.
- Pause or dispose offscreen animations, timers, and listeners. Cancel stale view-owned work on account changes; preserve valid domain operations until completion or explicit reset.
- Effective reduced motion is enabled when the OS requests it OR the app's "Reduce motion" preference is enabled. Never override an OS request by turning the app preference off.
- Reduced motion removes parallax, translation, scale, stagger, and decorative loops; use immediate updates or a brief opacity change where useful. Preserve all information and functionality.
- Haptics are optional, subtle, and preference-aware. Never use sound or haptics as the only signal.

Include a developer-accessible motion/component gallery so these patterns can be reviewed consistently. Do not claim smooth performance solely because animation code exists; inspect actual runtime behavior.

- A visit cannot close while actionable orders or an unsettled balance remain. Resolve/cancel/settle explicitly.
- Closing a visit preserves history and moves its table into needs-reset until staff mark it available.

ORDER LIFECYCLE

- Model draft, submitted, accepted, preparing, ready, and channel-specific served/collected/delivered/completed states.
- Add rejected and cancelled branches with clear eligibility and reasons.
- Enforce allowed transitions in shared local action handlers, not only by hiding buttons.
- Historical order lines preserve the purchased price/options even if the menu is later edited.
- Changing availability blocks new purchases; it does not silently remove an existing order.
- A paid rejected/cancelled order needs its own refund status; it must not simply become unpaid.

PAYMENT LIFECYCLE

- Keep checkout attempts and payment/refund records separate from food status.
- Freeze a cart snapshot and assign a stable attempt ID when checkout begins.
- Pending attempts do not silently absorb later cart edits.
- Repeated taps, retry callbacks, and restored screens cannot create duplicate orders, payments, tickets, or refunds.
- In pay-before mode, successful payment releases one order for acceptance; failed/cancelled/pending attempts do not create an actionable kitchen ticket.
- In pay-at-end mode, submitting an order can leave the bill unpaid while preparation progresses.
- Payment failure/cancellation preserves the draft and offers a new valid attempt.
- A pending attempt survives safe navigation and restart and can be resolved by a demo event.
- Successful payment reduces the correct bill balance once. Refunds update money records without rewriting preparation history.
- For this prototype, allow only one pending shared-bill checkout per visit. Show other participants that payment is in progress. Reserve the specific payable order amounts in its frozen allocation; release that reservation on failure/cancellation.
- If another round is added during pending payment, success settles only the frozen allocation and leaves the new round payable. Prevent separate attempts from settling the same amounts twice.
- Derive billable obligations, settled allocations, refunds, and outstanding balance separately. Cancelling/rejecting an order removes its billable obligation; refunding its earlier payment must not reopen that cancelled amount as money owed. Keep sales, captured payments, refunds, and outstanding balances distinct in reports.
- A simulated cash collection requires an explicit authorized staff action.
- Always identify the checkout as "Demo checkout — no real payment."

SHARED BEHAVIOR

- Restaurant/account switching cancels view-owned work only. Valid domain operations retain their original account, restaurant, visit, and attempt associations and may complete while another role is active. Global reset or scenario replacement explicitly invalidates old domain operations so late callbacks cannot alter the new demo world.
- Empty/error/offline states offer meaningful recovery; retry preserves the user's draft.
- Back navigation, Android back gesture, keyboard dismissal, and dirty-form confirmation work consistently.
- All primary actions change local state or open relevant content. Supporting controls are implemented, intentionally disabled with a reason, or omitted.
- "Coming soon" and generic snackbars are not substitutes for the requested core workflows.
