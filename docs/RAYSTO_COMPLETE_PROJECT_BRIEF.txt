Raysto coordinates a restaurant visit across diners, restaurant staff and AI.
Its central experience is an authorized table conversation connected to real
menus, reviewed orders, service requests, individual payment shares and a
restaurant workspace. PostgreSQL owns the business records. Gemini proposes
actions through authorized tools. People approve orders and their own payments.
PayPal provides the Sandbox checkout and payment infrastructure.

This brief separates three things: the approved product scope, implemented
source, and observed acceptance evidence. The current release is an integration
candidate. It is not a commercially activated marketplace or a completed
end-to-end payment release. A feature being present in source does not mean
every device, provider and recovery scenario has passed.

No private keys, access tokens, buyer passwords or server credentials belong in
this brief. Public resource identifiers and product endpoints are included where
they help explain the system. Current repository status documents take precedence
over older plans; some historical documents still describe superseded phases.

A. PROJECT IDENTITY AND POSITIONING

Name: Raysto.
Brand domain: rays.to.
Native package identity: com.gmrt.raysto.
Primary product: native Flutter mobile application for diners and restaurant
teams, supported by a connected Next.js restaurant console.
Backend: Serverpod with PostgreSQL, hosted on AWS.
Identity and supporting cloud services: Firebase and Google Cloud.
AI: server-side Gemini, with human approval for consequential actions.
Payments: PayPal Sandbox; live-money operation remains disabled.
Additional focused sponsor integration: AG Grid and AG Studio.

Short positioning:
“Raysto turns a shared restaurant visit into a coordinated conversation, from
choosing food together to paying individual shares through PayPal.”

Judge-facing positioning:
“An AI-assisted restaurant coordination system in which grounded recommendations
become reviewed orders, exact bill allocations and user-approved PayPal checkout,
with staff working from the same authoritative records.”

The distinctive unit is the visit: a temporary group, its staff audience, menu
context, preferences, purchases and financial obligations. A table can host many
visits over time. Each party receives its own private operational context.

The phrase “Road to 10/10” describes a quality target. It is not a predicted
judging score, a completion percentage or a guarantee of winning.


B. PROBLEM, AUDIENCE AND VALUE

The product hypothesis is that group dining creates several connected
coordination problems. People have different preferences and budgets; menu
availability changes; a waiter must translate conversation into actionable
service; kitchen progress has to reach the correct party; and a shared bill
eventually becomes several people's financial responsibility.

Raysto is intended to reduce repeated clarification and make the current state
understandable to everyone authorized to see it. Its value comes from carrying
context across these steps. A recommendation references menu records, an order
freezes the purchased details, a bill is derived from those records, and a
payment is reconciled against the exact amounts reserved for that payer.

For diners, the intended benefits are easier group decisions, visible service
progress, understandable shares and independent payment approval. For staff,
they are clearer requests, less duplicate communication and a shared operational
view. For owners, they are coordinated service, menu management and financial
visibility across restaurants.

These are hypotheses to validate with restaurant participants. Raysto currently
has no measured revenue uplift, time-saving percentage, customer adoption figure
or completed restaurant pilot to report.


C. USERS, ROLES AND PERMISSIONS

Guest: uses an authorized anonymous session for personal shopping and admitted
visits. Guests do not obtain staff roles. Claiming guest records after registration
requires proof of both sessions and a guarded ownership transfer.

Registered diner: manages personal records, preferences, carts, orders, favorites,
reservations, reviews and authorized room participation. Firebase identity is
checked separately from business authorization.

Table host: a visit participant with authority to confirm a reviewed group order.
The first admitted diner is the default host unless staff appoint another.
Host transfer is explicit and version checked. Being host does not authorize
another participant's PayPal payment or expose their unrelated records.

Owner: configures the restaurant, team, business settings, ownership and billing.
An owner can manage more than one restaurant. Sensitive ownership and payment
changes require stronger authentication.

Manager: manages menu and service operations, reports, operational invitations
and permitted refunds. A manager cannot freely elevate other users to owner or
manager. Refund authority includes recent-authentication checks.

Waiter: works with assigned tables and visits, responds to service requests,
assists with orders and records cash where authorized. Assignment matters in
addition to the general waiter role.

Kitchen: sees the ticket details needed for preparation and can perform permitted
availability/progress actions. This role does not need customer payment details
or unrelated delivery addresses.

Courier: sees assigned active deliveries, necessary delivery details and allowed
status changes. Consented location sharing is limited to the active assignment.

Platform administrator: handles explicitly authorized platform operations,
suspensions, financial exceptions, failed jobs and audit review. Administrator
access is not accepted from a client-supplied role or a preference document.

Every protected command must resolve the requesting actor and verify the
restaurant, target record, current role, assignment and allowed action. Hiding a
button is not the authorization mechanism.


D. PRODUCT PRINCIPLES AND BOUNDARIES

1. PostgreSQL is authoritative for permissions, prices, orders, table occupancy
   and financial records.
2. AI produces grounded suggestions and explicit proposals. It does not decide
   that an order is authorized or a payment has succeeded.
3. The host confirms the reviewed group order. Every payer approves their own
   exact payment independently.
4. Money uses integer minor units. A dollar amount of 13.50 is stored as 1,350
   minor units, avoiding binary floating-point money calculations.
5. Purchased names, choices, prices, fees and tax amounts are historical snapshots.
6. Unknown allergens and dietary facts stay unknown until supported by real data.
7. Shared-room audiences are visible; private help has a separate context.
8. The explicit offline demo is separate from connected identities and SQL data.
9. A browser return, animation, chat message or AI answer cannot settle a bill.
10. Source completion, verified functionality and deployment are separate claims.

The product covers dine-in, pickup and restaurant-managed delivery. It does not
currently include an independent delivery fleet, payroll, ingredient purchasing,
foreign-exchange conversion, voice calls or a social messaging network.

Flutter web is not a target. The web product uses React/Next.js. AWS is the chosen
host; Render is excluded. APIMatic and other sponsor integrations are deferred
to preserve focus on the core AI, PayPal and AG Studio journey.


E. THE COMPLETE RESTAURANT VISIT

The following is the intended complete journey. Selected steps already have
connected evidence; payment completion and the physical multi-person journey
still require acceptance.

1. A diner discovers a published restaurant, checks its services and menu, and
   chooses dine-in, pickup or delivery.
2. At the restaurant, staff seats the party or the diner uses the restaurant's
   permitted admission flow. Scanning the printed QR identifies the table.
   It does not reveal an occupied party's room or bill.
3. Admitted diners enter a visit-specific room with the assigned waiter. The
   interface clearly names the audience. Menu, Order and Bill remain pinned.
4. Participants share preferences voluntarily. Someone invokes the assistant:
   “Two vegetarian diners, food under $30, and something to share.”
5. Gemini reads authorized menu and preference facts, then requests a
   server-calculated quote. It returns an editable proposal with reasons,
   references, totals and any unknown dietary information.
6. Diners adjust the proposal. An unavailable item or changed price invalidates
   the relevant stale proposal; a new reviewed quote is needed.
7. The host approves the exact current revision. The backend rechecks the
   business conditions and creates the order. Kitchen staff receive structured
   tickets rather than the party's whole chat history.
8. The waiter acknowledges requests and the kitchen progresses the order.
   Relevant events appear in the room and authorized operational workspace.
9. Another round creates new charges. It does not rewrite a previously frozen
   checkout or equal-split plan.
10. A diner asks for the bill or a split. AI explains a proposal; deterministic
    backend code calculates the exact allocations and rounding.
11. The current payer reviews their amount and opens actual PayPal Sandbox
    approval. The backend captures only through the guarded payment path.
12. Verified provider processing updates the payment records and remaining
    balance. Other diners and staff receive authorized updates.
13. The next payer settles their previously frozen share. A failed or uncertain
    attempt is reconciled before its allocation can be reused.
14. Once orders, requests, attempts and balance are resolved, staff closes the
    visit and explicitly resets the table for a new party.

Observed QA example: the assistant produced a $27 vegetarian basket, the host
submitted it, and two $13.50 shares were proposed. An actual Sandbox approval
handoff was created. The latest documented QA ledger still has $27 outstanding,
zero settled and zero captures. This is not a completed payment example.


F. CUSTOMER MOBILE EXPERIENCE

The connected Flutter product covers discovery, restaurant details, menus,
modifiers, persistent baskets, authoritative quotes and order history. It also
provides table QR entry, visits, service requests, bills, reservations, waitlist,
private help and account-related operations.

Discovery uses published restaurant/menu records and bounded search. Service
mode, availability and location eligibility can constrain a purchase. Favorites
and verified-order reviews belong to the user; review moderation is separate
from the restaurant's ability to respond.

The basket validates required and optional choices, quantities, availability,
discounts, fees, configured taxes and tips. A quote is an authoritative snapshot
with a revision/expiry context. The client cannot submit a price of its own.

During an active dine-in visit, the conversation becomes the main workspace.
Before a visit, discovery remains useful. This keeps the conversation model
focused on an actual service context rather than turning the entire product
into a general-purpose messenger.

The app offers Google and email/password sign-in through Firebase. It obtains
a separate Serverpod session and stores backend credentials securely. Logout
clears coordinated session state; late responses cannot restore a logged-out
identity. Registered-account proof is refreshed for protected v2 operations.

The explicit offline mode contains illustrative restaurants, users and local
role simulations. It is useful for interface exploration. Its simulated AI,
staff permissions and payment outcomes do not establish connected functionality.

Android candidate: 0.11.5+2014, ARM64, minimum Android SDK 24, target SDK 36,
development signed for direct testing. It is not a Play Store release. iOS source
exists; a validated iOS binary and App Store release remain separate work.


G. CONVERSATIONS, ROOMS AND LIVE UPDATES

A table room belongs to a visit, not permanently to the physical table. Its
default audience is admitted diners plus the assigned waiter. A later party
receives another room. Moving tables preserves the visit and conversation.

The room includes human messages and structured operational cards: shared
preferences, basket proposals, item changes, service requests, order progress,
payment shares and receipts when backed by actual settlement. Adjacent updates
for the same record can be grouped without deleting the underlying history.

Message capabilities include replies, unread cursors, retries, delivery state,
encrypted local drafts and stable client message IDs. Repeating the same message
request must produce one persisted message. Persisted delivery does not imply
that a waiter has acknowledged or completed a request.

Membership controls history and future access. Leaving or deactivation removes
future access according to the role and room rules; it does not erase financial
obligations. Staff replacement is announced. A closed room is read-only for
authorized historical access.

Private help uses a separate authorized conversation. Its contents are not
included in a shared-room AI answer. Kitchen staff receive tickets, and couriers
receive delivery context, instead of unrestricted table conversations.

PostgreSQL stores rooms, members, messages, read cursors and ordered events.
Authenticated HTTP event streaming supports connected clients. Streams recheck
membership and account validity; reconnect uses cursors and authorized snapshots.
Polling is a fallback, not evidence that real streaming is functioning.

Observed QA included two diners and one waiter reading the same persisted
message, outsider denial, deduplicated retries and active-stream revocation.
One revocation observation ended access in 2.074 seconds. Two first-frame
measurements were 1.469 and 1.642 seconds. These are individual measurements,
not performance guarantees or load-test results.


H. RESTAURANT ONBOARDING, MENUS AND MANAGEMENT

The owner creates a draft restaurant and configures contact details, address,
timezone, currency, languages and services. Publication checks operational
readiness. Pauses and suspensions affect permitted new activity while preserving
access required to resolve existing obligations.

Service configuration covers weekly hours, special dates, temporary pauses,
preparation times, ordering cutoffs, pickup capacity, delivery areas, minimum
orders and fees. The server repeats critical availability checks when an order
is submitted; an earlier availability response is advisory.

The catalogue covers categories, items, descriptions, translations, photos,
prices, required/optional modifier groups, availability and optional sellable
quantity limits. Required modifier groups represent variant choices. Allergen
fields distinguish unknown information from known absence.

Menu editing supports drafts and version checks. Publication replaces the
current catalogue atomically and archives omitted entries while retaining
historical references. Existing purchases keep their frozen names and prices.
The initial bounded catalogue default is 200 current items per restaurant.

Floor plans include zones, stable table IDs, labels, capacities, shapes,
positions and rotation. Layout drafts carry versions to detect conflicting
changes. Printed QR identifiers resolve tables without granting party access.

Team administration includes invitations, acceptance, role activation,
deactivation, operational assignments, ownership transfer and audit history.
Until transactional email is configured, an authorized invitation can be
accepted through its manual code flow.


I. KITCHEN, RESERVATIONS, PICKUP AND DELIVERY

Order progression is guarded:
awaiting payment -> submitted -> accepted -> preparing -> ready ->
served / collected / delivered -> completed.

Rejection, cancellation and issues have explicit branches. Customers can cancel
before acceptance; later cancellation becomes a reviewed operational request.
Kitchen readiness is independent of financial status.

Pay-before orders become actionable only after verified payment. Pay-at-end
dine-in orders can be prepared while their bill remains unpaid. Item-level
kitchen progress and ticket projections provide the information required to
prepare and hand off the purchased food.

Reservations include availability, temporary holds, confirmation, modification,
cancellation, arrival, no-show and seating. Capacity and table conflicts are
checked atomically. Restaurant timezones are explicit; stored instants use UTC.
Waitlist offers have expiry and transition rules. Seating creates a fresh visit.

Pickup uses preparation estimates, scheduled capacity where configured and
collection confirmation. Delivery uses restaurant-managed couriers, eligibility
and fee rules, assignment, pickup handoff, completion and issue resolution.

An assigned courier can share consented location during active work. Location is
advisory and time limited; the security contract documents five-minute expiry.
Presence or location never grants assignment authority. Live phone GPS and
delivery-device behavior still need physical acceptance.


J. AI ORCHESTRATION, MENU IMPORT AND SPENDING

The current application configuration pins gemini-3.8-flash in Google's EU
multi-region. This is the recorded project configuration, not a claim that a
different model is currently inferior or that the chosen model is always
available. The latest documented provider blocker is HTTP 429 RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED.

The conversational assistant runs on the backend. It receives only the context
authorized for the requesting actor and room. Its bounded loop can call typed
tools to retrieve menu/preferences, obtain prices, inspect operational state,
read bills, prepare reviewed actions or investigate a permitted payment.

Core controls:
- Maximum four model rounds, eight tool calls and a 30-second run deadline.
- Tool authorization is repeated independently of the model's text or IDs.
- Streamed responses must be complete before any tool call is released.
- Proposals contain revision, actor, audience, relevant record versions,
  expiry and the exact proposed action.
- Approval is an explicit UI command bound to that proposal; a chat “yes” does
  not authorize payment, order publication or another person's share.
- Cancelled or stale runs cannot later publish an action.
- A retryable error can restore a question for review, without automatic paid
  retry or overwriting unfinished text.
- Validated read-only facts may be rendered directly without paying for another
  model paraphrase.

Example group request: “Four of us, two vegetarian, under $60, something shared.”
The intended response identifies supported menu items and modifiers, checks the
budget with the pricing service, explains tradeoffs and flags unknown facts.
Conflicting preferences or an impossible budget require correction or
clarification. The model cannot invent a price to make the constraint pass.

For unavailable items before submission, the proposal becomes stale and can be
revised. For accepted orders, staff must use the proper adjustment/cancellation
rules. AI does not silently substitute purchased food.

Bill questions can produce exact allocation proposals or explain the remaining
balance. Payment investigation is read-only. The assistant can report the actual
MCP or REST source used; it cannot declare success without provider evidence.

Contextual suggestions are restrained and deduplicated. Their approved scope is
unavailable items, explicit bill requests and unresolved payment outcomes.
Ordinary status updates do not need a model call. Participants can mute help.

Menu import supports text and uploaded image/PDF sources through a separate
pipeline: upload intent -> private quarantine -> validation/scanning ->
extraction -> structured draft -> human correction -> explicit publication.
JPEG, PNG, WebP and PDF inputs have byte/pixel/page limits. Initial defaults
include 8 MiB uploads, 20-page PDFs and 20-megapixel images. Missing prices,
ingredients and allergens remain unresolved fields, not guessed facts.

AI has no arbitrary SQL, unrestricted URL fetching, payment secrets, autonomous
capture/refund tool or direct publish authority. Document text is untrusted input.
Private-help and foreign-restaurant context remain outside shared-room runs.

Spending authorization is $50 total: $35 development/pilot and $15 reviewer
reserve. Atomic reservations occur before calls; usage reconciliation records
cost by run and capability. Unknown usage keeps a conservative reservation.
Insufficient remaining allowance stops new AI work while manual ordering and
payment remain available. Increasing this allowance requires owner approval.
The latest recorded development ledger was $2.850219; this is historical
application accounting, not the provider invoice or a freshly checked balance.

The fixed evaluation includes normal and impossible budgets, conflicting
preferences, unknown allergens, stale availability, French/Arabic requests,
privacy attacks, payment questions, odd-cent splits, truncated responses,
cancellation and exhausted budgets. The complete evaluation is still pending.


K. PAYPAL PAYMENTS, SHARED BILLS AND MCP

Raysto maintains separate configurations for restaurant payments, future
marketplace operation, platform subscription billing and the standalone Sandbox
test facility. One explicitly configured test restaurant can use the current
single-merchant Sandbox mode with zero commission. This does not simulate a
completed seller-onboarding approval.

Shared-bill options cover the whole outstanding bill, selected purchased units,
a custom amount and explicit per-charge amounts. AI can help describe the
selection, but deterministic code validates the payable balances and rounding.

Equal splits freeze every participant's original share. For a $27 two-person
split, each owes $13.50. After the first pays, the second still owes $13.50,
not half of the now-reduced balance. Odd cents are assigned deterministically
so that the sum of shares equals the original bill.

The financial sequence is:
1. Authorize the actor and target visit/order.
2. Lock the payable balance and reserve exact charges.
3. Persist the checkout and immutable allocation snapshot.
4. Contact PayPal outside the database transaction with stable request IDs.
5. Return the approval URL for the payer's explicit approval.
6. On completion, verify provider identity, merchant, currency, amount and
   Raysto reference before applying the result.
7. Persist captures/allocations and durable events once.
8. Use verified webhooks and reconciliation to recover missing or delayed state.

Only one unresolved checkout per visit is allowed. Other diners see the active
payment state and wait until settlement or safe reconciliation. New rounds stay
outside a frozen attempt. A timeout means the outcome may be unknown; it does
not automatically mean no charge occurred.

Cash collection uses the same locking and allocation rules and requires
authorized staff. Refunds cannot exceed captured, unrefunded allocations.
Credits adjust obligation; refunds adjust collected money. Cancelling an order
and returning money must not recreate cancelled debt. Disputes and reversals
require verified evidence and audited handling.

PayPal MCP has a specific operational purpose: investigating an authorized
order or refund. It is not the write path for checkout, capture or refunds.

Actual current MCP implementation:
- Official @paypal/mcp 1.8.1, pinned with @paypal/agent-toolkit 1.11.0.
- A private stdio subprocess in the Raysto backend, with isolated environment
  and no public MCP listener.
- MCP initialization, schema discovery and tool calls are actually performed.
- Only get_order(id) and get_refund(refund_id) are allowed.
- Provider IDs are resolved from authorized Raysto records, not arbitrary
  model-supplied PayPal identifiers.
- Response identity, amount, currency and merchant are validated.
- A 15-second deadline, bounded output and process cleanup constrain the reader.
- A failure may use an authorized REST read with the actual fallback labelled.

The planned hosted https://mcp.sandbox.paypal.com/http connection remains
blocked by access/protocol responses. The deployed alternative is official
self-hosted stdio. It must not be described as a repaired hosted connection.

A fresh unapproved $1 Sandbox order was read successfully through the deployed
Dart MCP implementation in 2.737 seconds, with no REST fallback or financial
mutation. The test checkout was then cancelled. A prior $13.50 direct read also
passed. Complete Gemini-to-MCP conversational acceptance and refund-detail
acceptance remain open.

The upstream MCP dependency audit is not clean: the October 27 report lists
13 affected package entries, including transitive advisories, with 12 high and
one moderate. That is not a count of 13 demonstrated reachable exploits, but
it remains a dependency-review task before widening use.

Live marketplace use additionally requires appropriate PayPal platform approval,
eligible countries/products, verified merchant connections and correct partner
configuration. Existing Sandbox success does not establish those capabilities.


L. SUBSCRIPTIONS, COMMISSIONS AND FINANCIAL REPORTING

The approved business model combines a subscription per restaurant with
configurable transaction commission. An owner operating several restaurants
has a separate subscription for each restaurant.

Restaurants choose EUR, USD or GBP. There is no automatic currency conversion.
The current implementation locks currency once an order exists; historical
financial values must not be reinterpreted through later settings changes.

Commission initially uses the food subtotal after discounts, excluding taxes,
delivery fees and tips. Rates are versioned and must be configured. Cash
collection is recorded, but automatic commission collection on cash is disabled.
No commercial commission rate or subscription price has been chosen in this brief.

Subscription source covers checkout, provider confirmation, renewal, failure,
cancellation, grace periods, entitlements and history. The default renewal-failure
grace period is seven days. Current provider-plan support is one indefinite
monthly cycle with an explicitly configured final price, not an invented free
trial, setup fee or tax configuration.

Expiration can block new paid-service activity while preserving the access needed
to finish existing operations, settle/refund payments and export records.
Raysto's own subscription sales require a separate billing-merchant configuration
from the marketplace/commission arrangement. Commercial activation remains pending.

Reporting separates booked sales, collected money, refunds, outstanding balances,
commission and subscription revenue. Totals are grouped by currency rather than
silently added across EUR, USD and GBP. A paid amount, a fulfilled order and a
booked sale are different measures.

Tax rates and policies require explicit commercial configuration. Delivery fees
currently use a configured final amount without a separate delivery-tax component;
this needs review against the applicable tax policy before commercial rollout.


M. CONNECTED RESTAURANT CONSOLE AND AG STUDIO

The connected console is a separate Next.js application at ops.rays.to/ops.
It reads the same authorized backend records used by native mobile. An earlier
browser-local prototype also exists and must remain explicitly distinguished.

The workspace combines an interactive floor/table view, AG Grid operational
records, a selected room/timeline, and three AG Studio custom widgets focused

owed money. The console selected the matching QA table and explained its unpaid

reload in the documented check.

N. ARCHITECTURE AND DEPLOYMENT

Raysto uses a modular monolith: one backend with clearly separated business
modules and bounded workers. This keeps a shared transaction boundary while
leaving future separation possible when measured demand justifies it.

Logical flow:

Flutter mobile / Next.js operations console
             |
     Cloudflare + HTTPS + Raysto Nginx site
             |
     Serverpod RPC + REST v2 + authorized event streams
             |
     Identity, permissions, validation and domain services
             |
     PostgreSQL authoritative records + durable jobs/events
             |
     Gemini / PayPal REST / official PayPal MCP / Firebase / configured SES

Recorded project toolchain: Flutter 3.47.8, Dart 3.13.7 and Serverpod 4.0.4.
These are recorded versions, not an assertion that they are the latest available
today. Dependency lockfiles and reproducible configuration define the project.

The existing AWS host is shared with Awhar production. Raysto has separate
systemd services/users, database/roles, Nginx sites, release directories and ports
18080–18082. Raysto work must not restart or alter Awhar, its database or global
SDKs. The shared PostgreSQL major version is preserved.

The isolated local development container is documented as PostgreSQL 18.6. That
must not be mistaken for proof of the AWS database's major version. Local test
databases are isolated; unrelated databases must never be reset for Raysto tests.

Backend and console 0.11.6 are documented as deployed. The Android candidate is
0.11.5+2014. The release record includes readiness and authenticated-read checks,
retained previous releases and a Raysto database backup. No fresh production
probe was needed to prepare this brief.

Domains have distinct jobs: api.rays.to serves the backend, ops.rays.to serves
the operations console, and rays.to is reserved for a landing page. Public web
ordering is a later connected milestone, not a Flutter web deployment.


O. DATA OWNERSHIP AND ENTITY RELATIONSHIPS

PostgreSQL: actors, teams, permissions, restaurant configuration, menus, carts,
quotes, tables, visits, rooms, orders, reservations, delivery, payments,
subscriptions, audit records, jobs, support and business notifications.

Firebase Authentication: registered identity and account recovery.
Firebase Storage: private files and approved published assets, with business
ownership and publication metadata recorded in PostgreSQL.
Firestore: private personal display preferences.
Realtime Database: advisory presence only.
PayPal: external payment evidence reconciled into Raysto's ledger.
Gemini: proposed explanations, structured drafts and reviewed actions.

Core relationships:
- A restaurant owns memberships, menus, tables, reservations and orders.
- A table hosts successive visits; only one active visit is permitted.
- A visit has admitted participants, a host, staff assignment, orders and a room.
- A room has authorized members, messages and read/event cursors.
- An order contains immutable purchased lines and may have kitchen/delivery work.
- Purchased units create charges; allocations reserve payable parts of charges.
- A checkout references its frozen allocations and verified provider records.
- Captures, refunds and commission entries remain distinct financial records.
- Assistant proposals reference a run, context, actor, versions and expiry.

The generated v2 inventory records 59 platform model/transport types, 80 commands
and 46 queries. These counts exclude some legacy/shared Serverpod entities and
are not a claim that 59 separate business tables exist. The complete inventory
is in the appendices; field definitions remain in data-dictionary.md.


P. APIS, COMMANDS AND CLIENT CONTRACTS

The backend supports generated Dart RPC alongside REST v2, generated TypeScript
contracts and OpenAPI. Shared domain services enforce the same rules. Existing
compatible IDs and provider references are preserved through additive evolution.

Business writes use explicit operations such as CreateQuote, SubmitOrder,
StartVisit, AllocatePayment and RequestRefund. Generic record editing cannot
bypass their business transitions. Commands include idempotency keys where
repetition can cause effects; concurrent edits carry expected versions.

Common response behavior includes stable error codes, field errors, request IDs,
conflict details and appropriate retry guidance. Lists are bounded. Reports
accept fixed names, permitted date ranges and allowlisted filters. Raw AG Grid
expressions or SQL from a caller are never executed as database instructions.

Representative resource routes:
POST /api/v2/rooms/open
GET  /api/v2/rooms/{id}
POST /api/v2/rooms/{id}/messages
POST /api/v2/rooms/{id}/read
POST /api/v2/rooms/{id}/preferences
POST /api/v2/assistant/runs
GET  /api/v2/assistant/runs/{id}
POST /api/v2/assistant/runs/{id}/cancel
POST /api/v2/assistant/proposals/{id}/approve
GET  /api/v2/operations/snapshot
POST /api/v2/operations/layout
PUT  /api/v2/media/{id}/content

The generic command surface remains /api/v2/commands/{operation}. Route aliases
reuse the same authorization handlers. Room message requests include a stable
client message ID; replies must reference a permitted message in the same room.

Queries generally use limits from 1 to 100 and monotonic cursor IDs. Documented
report ranges are bounded to 93 days. Streaming carries event IDs/versions;
clients reconnect or refresh a snapshot instead of treating a stream as a ledger.

Legacy mutation/payment routes reject v2 records when a compatibility adapter
cannot safely preserve the new ledger rules. This is preferable to silently
reinterpreting an old client's order or payment behavior.

R. SECURITY, PRIVACY AND CONSENT

The implemented security design combines actor authentication, current account
validity, tenant membership, record ownership, operational assignment, field
restrictions, expected versions and explicit business transitions. These layers
apply to RPC, REST, exports, streams, jobs and AI tools.

Restaurant roles live in SQL. A Firebase UID, preference document, UI role picker
or model-generated instruction cannot create a staff permission. Guest claiming
transfers personal records only, with proof of both sessions.

Sensitive operations require recent authentication; ownership/payment-setting
and platform-administration actions require an additional factor. Account
revocation must affect independent Serverpod sessions and active streams.

App Check is an additional abuse signal. It does not replace identity or record
authorization. Token-consumption/replay protection is not claimed for the current
Dart implementation.

Files use upload intent, exact byte count and digest, private quarantine,
content validation, fixed scanner execution and constrained decoding. Sanitized
menu images can be published; original PDFs remain private. Missing scanner or
invalid processing fails closed. File metadata alone is not proof of safe content.

The HTTP contract includes bounded commands/webhooks/uploads and pagination.
Rate limits consider actor, tenant and guest creation; matching edge limits are
part of deployment configuration. Secrets and unnecessary personal data are
excluded from operational/audit output.

Privacy workflows include profile export/deletion requests, expiring private
exports, retention policies and anonymized financial actor references where
records must be retained. Large histories can enter an explicit operator hold.
Financial/legal retention periods still need policy configuration before launch.

Shared dietary preferences are voluntary. Private support context is segregated.
Only assigned active deliveries expose necessary addresses/location. Pilot
recordings and quotations require participants' permission.

Source controls and selected tests do not establish a comprehensive security
certification, regulatory compliance or suitability for live money. Current
provider, dependency and device gaps are listed later in this brief.


S. RELIABILITY, CONCURRENCY AND RECOVERY

Critical changes use transactions, locks, unique constraints and version checks.
The initial implementation serializes important mutations with a restaurant
lock, favoring consistency over high write concurrency. Load limits must be
measured before scaling claims are made.

Business changes and durable events are stored together. Workers claim jobs
with leases, perform external work outside long database locks, and record
success or bounded retry. Dead letters and operator replay are auditable.

Idempotency protects retries. A user retry, connection loss or duplicate webhook
must not create duplicate orders, messages or settlement. Provider request IDs
supplement Raysto's own persisted request/response state.

Uncertain payment attempts retain their allocations. A provider timeout, missing
redirect or app restart triggers a state read/reconciliation path. It does not
authorize a replacement payment or pretend that the user was never charged.

Stock, capacity, seating, table movement and reservation overlap are checked
under the relevant transaction. One active visit per table and one unresolved
checkout per visit are separate invariants.

Deployment uses Raysto-specific releases, readiness checks, backups and rollback
targets. The remaining operations work includes an encrypted off-host backup
destination, restore rehearsal, alert routing and measured load behavior.

Useful operational signals include API latency/errors, database contention,
aged uncertain payments, failed webhook processing, overdue jobs, notification
failures and AI cost/capacity failures. Business records must survive replay and
rollback without deleting financial history.

Y. READY-TO-USE PROJECT DESCRIPTION AND DEMONSTRATION STORY

One sentence:

Raysto connects diners, staff and AI in one restaurant visit, turning shared
preferences into reviewed orders and exact PayPal payment shares.

Short pitch:

Group dining is a coordination problem: people choose together, staff need clear
requests, and one bill eventually becomes several payments. Raysto gives each
visit an authorized conversation with the diners and assigned waiter. Gemini
uses real menu and pricing tools to propose an order; the host reviews it before
submission. The same backend tracks service, calculates individual shares and
hands each payer to PayPal Sandbox for approval. A connected AG Studio workspace
shows the restaurant the same operational records. People remain in control of
orders and money while AI helps them understand and coordinate the next step.

Longer project description, safe for the current candidate:

Raysto is a native mobile restaurant platform built around the shared visit.
Customers can discover a restaurant, explore its menu and enter an authorized
table room with their party and assigned waiter. Menu, Order and Bill stay close
to the conversation, so a suggestion or service request has an operational
context rather than becoming another disconnected message.

The assistant is connected to server-side tools. It can retrieve permitted menu
facts, use voluntarily shared preferences, request authoritative prices and
prepare an editable basket. Proposals carry versions and expiry. The table host
must approve the reviewed order, and the backend checks it again before it
becomes kitchen work. Unknown allergens stay unknown, and a stale proposal
cannot silently purchase a replacement item.

At settlement, deterministic backend logic calculates exact shares. Each diner
reviews their own amount and approves through PayPal Sandbox. The financial
design uses allocation reservations, idempotency, provider verification and
reconciliation so that retries and interrupted responses cannot simply create
another payment. PayPal's official MCP server adds restricted order/refund
investigation, while financial writes remain in the guarded payment service.

Restaurant teams work from the same PostgreSQL records. The connected Next.js
console combines a table map, AG Grid, AG Studio custom widgets and a server-side
assistant. Managers can ask which tables requested the bill and still have an
outstanding balance, then inspect the corresponding room and records.

The current integration candidate has demonstrated shared rooms, real Gemini
basket/share proposals, reviewed order submission, actual Sandbox approval
handoffs, direct official MCP order reads and connected manager queries. Full
buyer capture/refund acceptance, physical-device coordination and the restaurant
pilot remain open. The project repository documents both evidence and gaps.
